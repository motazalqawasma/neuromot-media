## Facts (STEP 1 must re-check each with 2+ sources; PMC11578627 was behind a CAPTCHA at planning time)
1. Moniz presented cerebral angiography at the Neurological Society in Paris and the French Academy of Medicine in 1927 (Wikipedia; check a 2nd source, e.g. Frontiers Neuroanat 2017 doi:10.3389/fnana.2017.00081).
2. First tries with strontium and lithium bromide in 3 patients failed, one patient died; then 25% sodium iodide in 3 patients gave the first cerebral angiogram (Wikipedia; NEED 2nd source before using "one died").
3. Nominated twice for a Nobel for angiography; both times the reviewer (Jacobaeus) advised against (PubMed 9733316, Neurosurgery 1998;43(3):602-11 + Wikipedia).
4. 1949 Nobel Prize, shared with W.R. Hess, "for his discovery of the therapeutic value of leucotomy in certain psychoses" (Wikipedia + nobelprize.org).
5. Optional: shot several times by a patient in 1939 (Wikipedia only; footnote says the lobotomy-patient story has no evidence) = do NOT use unless a 2nd source; never say it was a lobotomy patient.
6. Check before use: which artery (carotid) and how he reached it; Thorotrast later (Wikipedia says he helped develop it).
