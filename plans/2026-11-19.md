## Facts (each must be re-checked by the night run with 2+ sources; use only what checks out)
1. Leonard Wood (1860-1927), US Army general, Army Chief of Staff, later Governor-General of the Philippines. (Wikipedia / MDedge Fed Pract 2017)
2. While in Cuba (around 1898-1902) he hit his head on a chandelier. Some writers think this injury was linked to the tumor; this is NOT proven. Say "he once hit his head hard" and "nobody can prove the link", never "the bump caused it". (MDedge Fed Pract 2017)
3. Years later he had a seizure and weakness on the LEFT side (reported 1904 in the Philippines). (Hektoen International, citing Ljunggren, J Neurosurg 1982;56(4):471)
4. February 1910, Johns Hopkins: Harvey Cushing removed a PARASAGITTAL MENINGIOMA. Heavy bleeding made him stop; he finished the removal days later (Hektoen says 4 days, under local anesthesia: re-check). Wood recovered well and went back to work. (MDedge; Hektoen/Ljunggren)
5. 1927: symptoms came back. Cushing (now at Harvard, Boston) operated again. Severe bleeding; Wood died (August 1927, re-check exact date). Cushing later said he should not have taken the case so quickly. (MDedge; Hektoen/Ljunggren)
6. Optional detail, only if a 2nd source confirms it: Wood was awake and asked Cushing to keep going for a fuller removal (source so far: one reader letter in Yale Medicine 2010 from memory; too weak alone).
7. Clinical point for the zoom: parasagittal = next to the midline, beside the big sinus (superior sagittal sinus) in the dura. That is WHY it bled so much: the tumor sits on the brain's biggest vein channel. (Standard neurosurgery fact; cite a textbook or review, e.g. StatPearls "Meningioma".)
