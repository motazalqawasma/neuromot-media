## Facts (checked 2026-11-15)
- W.J., a WWII paratrooper (Nature 2012 Wolman "A tale of two halves", doi 10.1038/483260a) and war veteran, 48 at testing, with severe seizures (PNAS 2014 Shen, doi 10.1073/pnas.1422335112; EBSCO).
- Surgery Feb 1962 at White Memorial, Los Angeles, by Philip Vogel and Joseph Bogen (EBSCO; Psychology Today 2014).
- Corpus callosum cut to stop seizures spreading (PNAS 2014). About 200 million fibers (EBSCO; textbook).
- Tests by Michael Gazzaniga, then a Caltech graduate student in Roger Sperry's lab: pictures to the right hemisphere (left visual field) -> said he saw nothing, but his left hand pointed to / pressed for the answer (PNAS 2014; Nature 2012).
- Classic paper: Gazzaniga MS, Bogen JE, Sperry RW. Some functional effects of sectioning the cerebral commissures in man. PNAS 1962;48(10):1765-1769. doi:10.1073/pnas.48.10.1765 (Caltech repository, open, not CC BY -> recreated title card, no page screenshot).
- Sperry: half of the 1981 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for his discoveries concerning the functional specialization of the cerebral hemispheres" (nobelprize.org).
- Avoid: the cause of W.J.'s head injury (rifle butt vs bomb, sources differ); "left brain/right brain personality" myths.
