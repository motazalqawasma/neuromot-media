# Plan for Thu 2026-11-12: BRAIN STORY #3, Phineas Gage (1848)

Status: planned 2026-11-10 (Tue) after the Rosemary Kennedy video. Motaz said: just tell me, don't wait.

## Why this story
- Most famous brain injury case in medical history; all-male story (no blur work needed).
- Taplio: H.M./Milner (1,206 likes) and Rosemary (345) show "true brain case" posts win. Gage is the next classic from the PLAN 14b idea list.
- Angle (neurology, not psychology): the frontal lobe controls planning and self-control; the iron destroyed most of the front of his LEFT frontal lobe; he could still walk, talk and see, but people said he was "no longer Gage". Then the twist: he later drove stagecoaches in Chile for about 7 years, so the brain partly adapted.

## Facts (checked 2026-11-10 with the Univ. of Akron Gage site by Malcolm Macmillan; re-check 1 more source before writing)
- 13 Sep 1848, near Cavendish, Vermont, Rutland and Burlington Rail Road; he was a construction foreman.
- Tamping iron: 3 ft 7 in long, 13.5 lb, 1.25 in wide, tapering to 0.25 in.
- Entered under the left cheekbone, exited the top of the head, landed 25-30 yards behind him. May not have lost consciousness.
- Doctor: John Martyn Harlow (Cavendish). Home to Lebanon, NH about 10 weeks later.
- Harlow: before = capable, well-balanced; after = fitful, irreverent, profane; friends: "no longer Gage".
- Could not get his foreman job back (1849). Barnum's American Museum (short), Currier's livery stable (Hanover NH, about 1.5 years), stagecoach driver in Valparaiso, Chile (about 7 years).
- About 1859 to San Francisco; seizures from Feb 1860; buried 23 May 1860.
- Iron and skull: Warren Anatomical Museum, Harvard Medical School.
- MYTHS to avoid: not a circus freak, not a drifter, not a drunk/psychopath (undocumented).
