## Facts (checked 2026-11-17; re-check before writing)
- Einstein died 18 April 1955 in Princeton. Pathologist Thomas Stoltz Harvey did the autopsy and removed the brain, against Einstein's wishes and without the family's permission at first (Live Science, "Where is Einstein's brain?").
- Harvey weighed it, photographed it from many angles, put it in formalin and cut it into about 240 blocks (PsyPost review; Live Science).
- He kept it for more than 40 years and gave the remains back to Princeton's hospital in 1998, 43 years later. 170 pieces are at Princeton; 46 slides are shown at the Mutter Museum, Philadelphia; many pieces are missing (Live Science).
- Harvey died in 2007; his family gave his photos and slides to the National Museum of Health and Medicine (PsyPost).
- Diamond 1985 (Experimental Neurology, doi:10.1016/0014-4886(85)90123-2): left parietal area had more glial cells per neuron than 11 male controls.
- Witelson 1999 (Lancet, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(98)10327-6): brain weight NOT exceptional; parietal lobes about 15% wider than controls (35 men, 56 women); unusual Sylvian fissure path.
- Falk, Lepore, Noe 2013 (Brain 136:1304-1327) described unpublished photos: extra ridges, e.g. a fourth gyrus in the frontal lobe.
- LIMITATION to say in the video: it is ONE brain, studied decades later with small control groups; nobody can say these differences made him a genius (critics compare it to phrenology).
- Avoid: "Einstein used only 10% of his brain" myth; any claim that Harvey was "arrested" or "lost his licence" (not checked); exact brain weight in grams (sources differ).
- Citation for the video card: Witelson SF, Kigar DL, Harvey T. The exceptional brain of Albert Einstein. Lancet 1999;353:2149-53. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(98)10327-6 (not open access -> recreated title card, no page screenshot).
