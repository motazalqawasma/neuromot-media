[serious] 1848. Doctors said this man was NO LONGER the same man. His name was Phineas Gage. Same man, or not? Tell me at the end… 1 or 2.
[matter-of-fact] He was a railroad foreman in Vermont. One afternoon, an explosion shot his iron rod… over 1 meter long, 13 pounds… straight through the front of his head.
[impressed] And he SURVIVED. He may not even have passed out. Honestly, I almost pass out in anatomy lab.
[curious] But his doctor, John Harlow, noticed something strange. Before: careful, a great planner. After: rude, impatient, full of plans he never finished. His friends said he was "no longer Gage".
[matter-of-fact] The iron destroyed part of his LEFT frontal lobe… the part that plans, and holds you back. Save that. It's on every neuro exam.
[warmly] Here's the twist. Later, he drove stagecoaches in Chile for YEARS. A job that needs planning. So… was he still the same man? 1 for yes, 2 for no. Send this to your study partner, and see if they agree. Dr. Mot, one brain story at a time.
