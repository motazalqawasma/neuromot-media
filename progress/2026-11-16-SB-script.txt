Brain Story #6 - W.J., the first split-brain patient tested (1962), Gazzaniga, Bogen, Sperry (Motaz's cloned voice, eleven_v4)

[curious] Quick... which hand is holding your PHONE right now? 1 = left. 2 = right. Keep that answer.
[matter-of-fact] Los Angeles, 1962. A war veteran had seizures that nothing could STOP. So surgeons cut the bridge between his two brain halves... the corpus callosum. About 200 MILLION nerve fibers.
[curious] The seizures calmed down. And in everyday life... he seemed totally NORMAL.
[impressed] Then a young scientist, Michael Gazzaniga, flashed pictures to ONE half at a time. The right half? He said, "I saw NOTHING." But his left hand... pointed at the answer anyway.
[serious] His left hand knew something... his MOUTH didn't. Basically two roommates who stopped texting each other.
[warmly] Save this: each hand is run by the OPPOSITE brain half, and speech usually lives on the left. This work won Roger Sperry the 1981 NOBEL Prize. So... which hand has your phone? 1 or 2. Send this to your left-handed friend. Dr. Mot, one brain story at a time.
