[matter-of-fact] Paris, 1840. A 30-year-old craftsman suddenly loses his SPEECH. He goes into Bicetre Hospital... and never comes out. Everyone just calls him "Tan".
[curious] But here's the thing. He UNDERSTOOD almost everything. He could read the time on a watch. Honestly... better than me before an 8 a.m. lecture.
[serious] After about 10 years there, his RIGHT side became paralyzed. In April 1861, he met a surgeon, Paul Broca. Days later... he died.
[impressed] Broca opened his brain and found the damage in the LEFT frontal lobe. Today we call it BROCA'S area. Save that. Speech OUTPUT. Exam classic.
[warmly] The twist? For 150 years, nobody knew his NAME. In 2013, a Polish historian found it. Louis Victor Leborgne. So... lose speech, or lose movement? 1 or 2. Send this to the friend who still mixes up Broca and Wernicke. Dr. Mot, one brain story at a time.
